\titledquestion{Implicit differentiation of a PDE}

Consider the one-dimensional Poisson equation
\[
    -u''(x) = \alpha, \qquad x \in (0,1),
    \qquad u(0)=u(1)=1,
\]
where $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ is a parameter. We discretize the interval at
$x_i=i/10$, for $i=0,\ldots,10$, and denote by
$\boldsymbol{u}(\alpha)=(u_1(\alpha),\ldots,u_9(\alpha))^\top$
the values at the nine interior points. The standard centered finite-difference
discretization gives the implicit equation
\[
    A\boldsymbol{u}(\alpha)=h^2\alpha\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{b},
    \qquad h=\frac{1}{10},
\]
where $\boldsymbol{1}\in\mathbb{R}^9$ is the vector of ones and
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
  2&-1& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\
 -1& 2&-1& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\
  0&-1& 2&-1& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\
  0& 0&-1& 2&-1& 0& 0& 0& 0\\
  0& 0& 0&-1& 2&-1& 0& 0& 0\\
  0& 0& 0& 0&-1& 2&-1& 0& 0\\
  0& 0& 0& 0& 0&-1& 2&-1& 0\\
  0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0&-1& 2&-1\\
  0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0&-1& 2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol{b}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The vector $\boldsymbol{b}$ contains the contribution of the two boundary
values. You may assume that $A$ is invertible.
Find the derivative of the value at the center of the interval with respect
to $\alpha$, that is, compute \( \frac{\mathrm{d}u_5}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}. \)

\begin{solutionbox}{10cm}
    Let
    \[
        F(\boldsymbol{u},\alpha)
        =A\boldsymbol{u}-h^2\alpha\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{b}.
    \]
    Since $F(\boldsymbol{u}(\alpha),\alpha)=\boldsymbol{0}$, implicit
    differentiation gives
    \[
        A\frac{\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}
        -h^2\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{0}.
    \]
    Hence the derivative vector $\boldsymbol{v}
    =\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}/\mathrm{d}\alpha$ is obtained by solving the
    linear system $A\boldsymbol{v}=h^2\boldsymbol{1}$.

    For this particular system, it is easy to obtain a closed-form solution,
    even though this was not required to get the points. The entries
    \[
        v_i=\frac{x_i(1-x_i)}{2}
        =\frac{i(10-i)}{200}, \qquad i=1,\ldots,9,
    \]
    satisfy this linear system. Indeed,
    $2v_i-v_{i-1}-v_{i+1}=h^2$, with $v_0=v_{10}=0$.
    Therefore
    \[
        \boxed{\frac{\mathrm{d}u_5}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}
        =v_5=\frac{5(10-5)}{200}=\frac18.}
    \]
\end{solutionbox}
