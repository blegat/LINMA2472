\titledquestion{Sparse Jacobian recovery}

Let $n\geq 3$ and consider the function
\[
    F:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^n,
    \qquad
    F(x)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        x_1^2+\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^n x_j^2\\
        c_2\exp(x_1)\\
        \vdots\\
        c_n\exp(x_1)
    \end{pmatrix},
\]
where $c_2,\ldots,c_n$ are nonzero constants.

Assume that the sparsity pattern of the Jacobian is known and that
$x_i\neq 0$ for every $i$. A Jacobian-vector product (JVP) with seed $v$ is
$J_F(x)v$, and a vector-Jacobian product (VJP) with seed $w$ is
$w^\top J_F(x)$. Each product may use an arbitrary seed vector.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If only JVPs may be used, what is the minimum number of products
    required to recover the complete Jacobian? Justify your answer.
    \begin{solutionbox}{5.5cm}
        Every pair of columns has a structural nonzero in the first row.
        Hence no two columns are structurally orthogonal and they must have
        different colors. Therefore, $\boxed{n}$ JVPs are required.
    \end{solutionbox}

    \item If only VJPs may be used, what is the minimum number of products
    required to recover the complete Jacobian? Justify your answer.
    \begin{solutionbox}{5.5cm}
        Every pair of rows has a structural nonzero in the first column.
        Hence no two rows are structurally orthogonal and they must have
        different colors. Therefore, $\boxed{n}$ VJPs are required.
    \end{solutionbox}

    \item Can fewer Jacobian products be used if JVPs and VJPs are combined?
    If so, give suitable seed vectors and explain how the complete Jacobian
    is recovered.
    \begin{solutionbox}{12cm}
        Yes. Let $e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)^\top$. One JVP gives
        \[
            J_F(x)e_1=J_F(x)_{:,1},
        \]
        which is the complete first column. One VJP gives
        \[
            e_1^\top J_F(x)=J_F(x)_{1,:},
        \]
        which is the complete first row. All entries outside this row and
        column are known to be zero, so these two products recover the whole
        Jacobian:
        \[
            \boxed{J_F(x)=
            \begin{pmatrix}
                2x_1&2x_2&\cdots&2x_n\\
                c_2\exp(x_1)&0&\cdots&0\\
                \vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
                c_n\exp(x_1)&0&\cdots&0
            \end{pmatrix}.}
        \]
        Thus only $\boxed{2}$ Jacobian products are needed: one JVP and one
        VJP.

        One product cannot suffice: a single JVP does not distinguish the
        $n-1$ entries in the first row, while a single VJP does not
        distinguish the $n-1$ entries in the first column.
    \end{solutionbox}
\end{enumerate}
